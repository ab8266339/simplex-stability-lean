Explicit Stability of the Weak Simplex Bound
Research draft
6 November 2026
Abstract
Equal-energy Gaussian signaling uses the regular simplex as an optimal reference when
the signal dimension permits it. Quantifying geometric recovery from a small decoding
deficit requires a bound that remains valid near singular Gram matrices. We give explicit
global quadratic bounds for Gram and vertex distance at every fixed positive channel
parameter, together with a separate centroid penalty. A configuration-metric expansion
separates two in-plane sectors from a transverse centroid sector and identifies the local
least-loss directions. We prove unique ordered channel-parameter crossings and identify
the exact minimizers on all sufficiently small Procrustes shells in the strict-normal regime,
uniformly in the ambient dimension. Their minimal deficit has an explicit quartic expansion
with a sixth-order remainder. The global bounds, mean-width stability, transition and
small-shell results, including the remainder, are formalized in Lean. For four- and 16-signal
constellations, directional coefficients predict central perturbation losses more accurately
in aggregate than two scalar controls across 108 tested cells. A separate 20-run learning
comparison attributes improved matched-decoder performance to ordinary simplex Gram
regularization, rather than to a new optimization method. The evaluated global recovery
constants range from approximately 10−39 to 10−234 and give no practical finite-precision
geometry certificate. The results provide an explicit mathematical rate and a directional
local diagnostic, while practical certification requires sharper constants and effective
numerical bounds for the local radius and remainder constant.

Local optimality of simplex codes is classical. Litvak attributes fixed-parameter local
maximality to Balakrishnan [4, Theorem 2.5], whose original article is [8]. Weber already
expresses the general second variation through repeated, adjacent and disjoint edge pairs [12,
Lemma 5.8, equation (5.102)]. His three quadratic invariants are the squared edge norm,
squared total row sum and sum of squared row sums. On his feasible zero-total-sum face,
the row sums vanish and (5.105) gives the restricted form used in Theorem 5.5. Equations
(5.107)–(5.108) integrate the distribution-function variation into the decoding functional. Thus
edge-type decomposition and local simplex optimality are established antecedents of the
calculation below. We claim no priority for these local ideas or for the existence of a Hessian.
Our global quantitative contribution is the specified rate and its conversion into configuration
distance. The local formulas supply explicit normalization and feasible-sequence criteria for
using that rate. Section 7 additionally proves unique channel transitions and an exact, rather
than only second-order, optimizer on small shells in the strict-normal regime. Its complete
analytic and geometric proofs are in Appendices A and B; the precise scope of the Lean
verification is given in Appendix C.

Proposition 1 (What compactness supplies). Let K be a compact metric space, Z ⊂K a
nonempty closed set, and f : K →[0, ∞) continuous with zero set exactly Z. Then f(xj) →0
implies d(xj, Z) →0. If, in addition, f(x) ≥a d(x, Z)2 whenever d(x, Z) < ρ, with a, ρ > 0,
then there exists b > 0 such that f(x) ≥b d(x, Z)2 on K.
Proof. For each ϵ > 0, compactness gives a positive minimum of f on {d(x, Z) ≥ϵ} whenever
that set is nonempty. This proves the first assertion. For the second, let M = maxK d(x, Z). If
M = 0 or the set {d(x, Z) ≥ρ} is empty, the assertion is immediate. Otherwise its minimum
η is positive, and b = min{a, η/M2} works.
For fixed m, the correlation-matrix domain is compact. The analogous vertex formulation
uses a fixed ambient space and the closed simplex orbit. Thus a nonexplicit global quadratic
bound is a standard consequence of uniqueness and a local quadratic bound in the same
metric. Uniqueness and compactness alone give no power law. Nor are the Gram and vertex
metrics locally equivalent at the rank-deficient simplex, as Section 6 shows through an explicit
transverse example. These distinctions delimit the role of our global proof and constants.

A fixed-budget learning comparison
We adapt the communication autoencoder of O’Shea and Hoydis [13, Section III-A and Table
IV] to the two dimensions above. The encoder uses a dense layer of width m with rectified
linear activation, a linear layer of width d and per-codeword energy normalization. The
decoder uses a dense layer of width m with rectified linear activation and a softmax output of
width m. Training minimizes cross-entropy at Eb/N0 = 7 dB, where Eb/N0 = λ2/(2 log2 m).
An additional arm uses the ordinary simplex Gram mean-square penalty with weight 0.1.
This penalty follows from the known optimal geometry. The local decoding Hessian is not a
cross-entropy Hessian or an Adam preconditioner.
Five paired initializations per dimension use identical message and noise streams. Both
arms use double precision and Adam with learning rate 0.001 for 1200 steps. The batch size
is 256 for the first 600 steps and 1024 for the remaining 600 steps. Every run contributes
its final checkpoint, with no seed or checkpoint selection. These 20 runs complete a fixed
budget, without an optimization-convergence claim. The dimensions differ from the original
autoencoder’s (16, 7) example.

9
Conclusion and limitations
The explicit comparison controls Gram defect and centroid displacement, including singular
configurations. Together they give a global quadratic configuration bound at fixed m and
positive λ. The local expansion identifies the feasible least-loss sectors in the configuration
metric. Numerical diagnostics support its aggregate use for small central perturbations of four-
and 16-signal constellations. The normalized Gaussian moment family has three unique ordered
crossings for m ≥4 and a separate unique crossing for m = 3. In the strict-normal regime the
finite-radius small-shell optimizer is exactly the common-normal deformation, up to orthogonal
equivalence. Its deficit has a quartic expansion with a quantified O(ρ6) remainder and no
odd Taylor terms. The radius and remainder constant depend only on m and λ, not on the
embedding dimension. The global comparison, recovery and mean-width results, together with
the transition, strict-normal optimization and remainder results, have been checked in Lean
18


over the actual Gaussian integrals. The global constants are included; numerical diagnostics
and experimental claims remain outside the certificate.
The global constants deteriorate rapidly with the number of signals. Their numerical values
provide no practical geometry certificate in the tested cases. Practical recovery needs sharper
constants, an effective numerical lower bound for the local transition radius, computable
remainder bounds, and controlled integration error. The fixed-budget learning comparison
concerns known Gram regularization and matched decoding. Its decoder and convergence
limitations remain separate from the mathematical stability result. The rank penalty quantifies
a gap from the unrestricted simplex value, without identifying the optimal rank-constrained
constellation. The channel model assumes equal message probabilities and isotropic Gaussian
noise. Fading, synchronization, channel estimation and constrained modulation alphabets
require separate analysis.
